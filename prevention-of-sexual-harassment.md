# Prevention of Sexual Harassment

## What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment is any unwelcome behaviour of a sexual nature. It does not matter if the person did not mean any harm. What matters is that the other person did not want it. Examples are:

* Unwanted touching, hugging or standing too close.
* Sexual comments or jokes, or comments about someone's body or looks.
* Staring in an uncomfortable way, or making gestures.
* Asking for sexual favours, or asking someone out again and again after they have said no.
* Sending inappropriate messages, photos or videos, or showing such material.
* Promising a benefit, like a promotion, or threatening a person at work in return for sexual favours.
* Following or stalking someone, or continuing to contact them when they do not want it.
* Spreading rumours about someone's personal life.

Harassment can happen in person, online, on calls and at any place that is connected to work.

## What would I do if I face or witness such behaviour?

If it happens to me:

1. Clearly tell the person to stop, if I feel safe to do so.
2. Write down what happened, with date, time, place, what was said and the names of any witnesses. I will also keep screenshots of messages.
3. Report it to the Internal Committee, HR or a senior person I trust, as given in the company PoSH policy.
4. Take support from a friend, family or a counsellor, because it can be stressful.

If I see it happening to someone else:

1. If it is safe, I will step in and stop the situation or support the person.
2. Later, I will check on the person and ask what help they need.
3. I will support them in reporting and tell the committee what I saw, if they allow it or when it is needed.
4. I will never ignore it or laugh along, and I will not spread the matter as gossip.

If it happens again and again, I will report it as soon as possible with all the notes that I have. Raising a complaint in good faith should not lead to punishment of the person who complains.
