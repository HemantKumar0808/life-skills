# Listening and Assertive Communication

## Question 1: Steps and strategies for Active Listening

1. Give full attention to the speaker and do not get distracted by my own thoughts.
2. Do not interrupt. Let the speaker finish what they want to say.
3. Use door openers, which are small phrases like "tell me more" or "that sounds interesting", so the speaker feels free to continue.
4. Show that I am interested with my body language, like eye contact, nodding and facing the speaker.
5. Repeat the main point in my own words (paraphrasing) to confirm that I understood it correctly.
6. Take notes when the talk is important, so that I do not forget the details later.

## Question 2: Key points of Reflective Listening according to Fisher's model

* Listen more and talk less.
* Respond to the personal side of what the speaker says, for example their feelings, and not to abstract or distant things.
* Do not argue, judge or give advice too early.
* Try to understand the speaker's point of view, and say it back to them in my own words so that they can confirm it.
* Pay attention to the tone of voice and body language along with the words.
* Reduce distractions so that the focus stays on the conversation.

## Question 3: Obstacles in my listening process

* My phone. When a notification comes, my attention goes to it immediately.
* Thinking about my reply while the other person is still talking, so I miss the last part of what they said.
* Being tired or hungry, because then I cannot concentrate for long.
* Sometimes I feel I already know what the person will say, so I stop listening properly.
* Noisy places and long online meetings.

## Question 4: What can I do to improve my listening?

* Keep my phone on silent and away during conversations and meetings.
* Wait for the person to finish before I think about my answer.
* Write short notes in meetings and technical discussions.
* After the discussion, repeat the main points back, like "So you want the feature ready by Friday, right?"
* Ask a clear question when I do not understand, instead of guessing.
* Sleep well and take small breaks, so that my mind stays fresh.

## Question 5: When do I switch to the Passive communication style?

I become passive when I am talking to seniors or people I have just met. I often do not say that I disagree, or that I did not understand something, because I do not want to look bad or start a problem. Sometimes I also say "okay, no problem" to a friend when I actually did not like something.

## Question 6: When do I switch to the Aggressive communication style?

It usually happens when I am stressed, tired, or running behind on a deadline. If someone keeps asking me the same thing again and again, my tone becomes harsh and I speak louder than needed. After that I usually feel bad about it.

## Question 7: When do I switch to the Passive Aggressive communication style?

I sometimes give short replies like "fine" or "whatever you want" when I am actually upset but do not want to say it. With close friends, I may make a sarcastic joke instead of saying clearly what bothered me. Sometimes I stay quiet and avoid the person for a while.

## Question 8: How can I make my communication assertive?

* Say what I think and need in a clear and calm way, using "I" sentences, for example "I need more time for this task" instead of blaming someone.
* Say no politely when I cannot do something, without feeling guilty and without a long excuse.
* Take a short pause before replying when I am angry.
* Keep my voice steady and my body language open.
* Respect the other person's side too, and listen to it fully before I answer.
* Speak up in the moment, and not keep things inside until they turn into anger or sarcasm.
* Practise with small things daily, such as asking a question in a meeting.
