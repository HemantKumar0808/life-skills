# Learning Process

## Question 1: What is the Feynman Technique?

It is a method where I pick a topic, try to explain it in very simple words like I am teaching a small child, and then go back and read again wherever I got stuck.

## Question 2: What was the most interesting story or idea in the Barbara Oakley video?

The most interesting part for me was her own story. She was not good at maths and science in school. She studied languages and even worked in the army. After that she learned engineering and became a professor. This made me feel that anybody can learn a hard subject if they use the right method, so I should not say "I am not good at this" too early.

I also liked the idea about procrastination. When we think about a work we do not like, our brain feels pain, so we run away and open the phone. The Pomodoro method fixes this. We just work for 25 minutes without any distraction and then take a short break. We only think about the 25 minutes, not about the whole work, so starting becomes easy.

## Question 3: What are active and diffused modes of thinking?

* Active mode (focused mode): We use this when we sit and concentrate on one problem, for example when we are solving a coding question or reading a new concept.
* Diffused mode: This is when our mind is relaxed and not focused on anything, for example when we walk, take a shower, rest or sleep. In this mode the brain connects ideas in the background, so sometimes the solution comes suddenly.

For hard topics, we need both. First we try hard in active mode, and then we take a break so that the diffused mode can help us.

## Question 4: Steps to take when learning a new topic (Learn Anything in 20 Hours)

1. Break the skill into small parts.
2. Learn just enough to correct your own mistakes.
3. Remove the things that stop you from practising.
4. Practise for at least 20 hours.

## Question 5: What can I do to improve my learning process?

* Whenever I learn a new concept, I will try to explain it in my own words, either by writing or by speaking loudly, like the Feynman Technique. If I cannot explain a part, I will study that part again.
* I will keep my phone on silent and away from my desk while I work. I will also turn off notifications except for work apps.
* I will use the Pomodoro method, which is 25 minutes of work and then a short break.
* I will not only read a concept. I will write my own code for it, because the bootcamp wants me to know how to implement it.
* After studying, I will close the notes and try to remember the main points, instead of reading the same thing again and again.
* For every topic I will ask these four questions: Where is it used? What is it, and can I explain it? How can I code it? By when should I finish it?
* I will sleep on time and exercise, because the video says it helps the brain to remember what we learn.
* If I am stuck on something, I will tell my mentor early and clearly, instead of waiting for many hours.
* I will track my time with an app, so that I can see how much time I really give to focused work.
